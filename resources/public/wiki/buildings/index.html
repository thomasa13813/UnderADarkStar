Buildings

Every building in Under a Dark Star - Population Centers, Industrial Zones, Spaceports, Defense Posts, Stargates and Shield Arrays - with costs, levels and rules.

Buildings turn territory into power. This page covers every building a challenger can raise in Under a Dark Star: what it does, what it costs, what each level adds, and how it is lost.

Troop numbers are as shown in game. One second of game time is ten ticks.

2. Rules every building follows

2.1 Placing

  • You build on your own nebula. The building snaps to the free tile nearest your click, up to 15 tiles away through your own territory.
  • Spacing: no building may stand within 15 tiles of any other building, yours or anyone else's, finished or not.
  • Nothing is ever built on the Dark Star, not even by an invader holding its tiles. The Hegemon builds nothing of its own anywhere; it can only gift (§2.7).
  • Clicking near one of your own buildings of the same type upgrades it instead of placing a second one. "Near" means within 15 tiles.
  • A Shield Array is the exception: it is built only on one of your own Defense Posts, which it upgrades (§3.6). Click on or near the post.
  • You cannot build during the spawn phase.
  • There is no limit on how many of any building you may own.

2.2 Prices climb

Population Centers, Industrial Zones, Spaceports and Defense Posts get dearer the more you have. Stargates and Shield Arrays never do.

  • The count is levels, not buildings. A level-3 Population Center counts three, so upgrading costs the same as building new at that count. The first upgrade of your only Population Center costs 250k.
  • Spaceports and Industrial Zones share one count. With one of each, the next of either costs 500k.
  • A building still under construction counts one.
  • Captured buildings do not raise your prices. Only what you built or upgraded yourself counts.
  • Losing buildings lowers your prices again, whether they were destroyed, captured or deleted.
  • A Shield Array counts as a Defense Post towards the post's price (it is one, upgraded).
  • Prices stop climbing at the cap: 1M for the three economic buildings, 250k for Defense Posts.

2.3 Building and upgrading

  • Gold is paid up front. The building appears at once, under construction.
  • A building under construction does nothing. It adds no troops, gives no defence bonus, sends nothing, launches nothing, shields nothing, and cannot be upgraded. It still blocks the 15-tile spacing.
  • Upgrades are instant. There is no construction time for a new level.
  • You can buy up to 50 levels in one order. Each step is priced at the climbing count, and the order stops at the first step you cannot afford.
  • There is no level cap on any upgradable building.
  • There is no cancel and no refund.

2.4 Deleting your own building

  • You may delete one of your buildings once every 30 seconds. It is marked, and it disappears 30 seconds later with all its levels. There is no refund.
  • A marked building cannot be upgraded, and a marked Spaceport no longer trades or serves Destroyers.
  • If an enemy captures the tile first, the deletion is cancelled and they keep the building.

2.5 Losing a building

  • Conquest: when an enemy takes the tile, they capture the building with every level intact. A half-built building keeps building, for them. A Defense Post is the exception: it is destroyed, not captured — unless it carries a Shield Array (§3.6), which is captured intact.
  • Neutral ground: a building on a tile that becomes nobody's is destroyed.
  • Elimination: all your buildings are destroyed.
  • Warheads: everything within the blast's outer radius is destroyed outright, all levels, whoever owns it, the launcher's own included (Gamma Ray Burst 100 tiles, Antimatter Reaction 10, which also erases the ground into deep space). The only exception is a building inside another player's Shield Array, which keeps out a Gamma Ray Burst but not a Reaction (§3.6).
  • The Hegemon's strike: a strike's bubble captures the buildings it rolls over (posts are destroyed). Retake the tile while the bubble lasts and you get the building back, levels intact. When the bubble pops, whatever buildings it still holds are destroyed.
  • Buildings have no health. Nothing else damages them.

2.6 Lords and Barons

Lords build with the same rules and prices as you (§6). Barons do not build.

2.7 Gifts from the Hegemon

The Hegemon may place any building a challenger could build on any challenger's territory (Barons included), or upgrade one already there. It pays from its own gold, and the host cannot refuse.

  • The Hegemon always pays the first-building price, for every level: 125k for a Population Center, Industrial Zone or Spaceport, 50k for a Defense Post, 500k for a Stargate, 2M for a Shield Array (which needs one of the host's Defense Posts to stand on: gift the post first).
  • The gift counts as the host's own build, so the host's next one costs them more.
  • A gift follows every placement rule of §2.1 and is under construction like any build.

3. The buildings

3.1 Population Center

Raises your troop ceiling.

  • Each level adds +25,000 max troops. Levels add up: a level-4 center is +100,000.
  • Your ceiling without centers is about 200 × tiles^0.6 + 10,000. A few points:
    • 1,000 tiles gives about 22,600.
    • 10,000 tiles gives about 60,200.
    • 50,000 tiles gives about 135,000.
  • For a small or blocked player, one center is a very large share of the ceiling. At 1,000 tiles a single level more than doubles it.
  • A center adds no troops per second directly. Troops regrow faster the further you are below your ceiling, so a higher ceiling keeps regrowth up for longer.
  • A trade stop. A center within 110 tiles of an Industrial Zone (anyone's) joins the trade network, and every supply delivery passing through it pays gold (§3.2). Its level does not change the payment.
  • A target. The Hegemon weighs challengers by their troop ceiling. An AI Hegemon's levelling strike aims at the strongest challenger's highest-level Population Center.
  • Lords: the ceiling is scaled by difficulty (Easy ×0.5, Medium ×0.75, Hard ×1, Impossible ×1.25). Barons: ÷3. The Hegemon: centers do not apply; its ceiling is fixed for the round.

3.2 Industrial Zone

Builds a trade network and sends supply deliveries along it.

  • The network. When finished, the zone links by straight trade lanes to Population Centers, Spaceports and other Industrial Zones within 110 tiles (but more than 15), anyone's.

    • A lane is a straight line. It fails if it crosses void, the Dark Star's disc, or open deep space. Only a narrow strait of shoreline space can be crossed, and each space tile on it counts as six toward the ~155-tile length limit.
    • No duplicate links. A station does not link to one it can already reach in three hops or fewer.
    • Snapping. A building placed on an existing lane (within 3 tiles) splits it and joins.
    • Late joiners. A Population Center joins only if an Industrial Zone is within 110 tiles when it is finished, or one is built near it later. A Spaceport keeps checking until it joins.

  • Supply deliveries. Only Industrial Zones send them.

    • Odds. Each zone gets one chance a second per level (§5.1 has the odds).
    • Needs a paying stop. A zone with no Population Center or Spaceport on its network, or none it may trade with, sends nothing.
    • Route. A delivery picks a random Population Center or Spaceport on its network and travels there by the fewest stops.

  • Gold is paid at every Population Center and Spaceport the delivery passes, the destination included. Industrial Zones on the route pay nothing.

    Stop belongs toThe delivery's owner getsThe stop's owner gets
    You10,000—
    A teammate or a neutral player25,00025,000
    An ally35,00035,000
    • Long routes still pay. From the 11th paying stop on a route, each pays 5,000 less than the last, down to a floor of 5,000.
    • No embargoes. A delivery never pays at, or travels to, a player either side has stopped trading with. It ends where the next stop is barred.

  • Armies. Each level of a finished zone supports one Army (§3.8).

  • Level means more deliveries (not more gold per delivery) and one more Army.

  • More zones, each a little slower. Each extra zone you own makes every one of your zones slightly less likely to send. Total output still grows, but less than proportionally (§5.1).

3.3 Spaceport

Sends freighters to other players' spaceports, and launches and repairs Destroyers.

  • Placed anywhere in your territory, like a Population Center. Its harbour is the nearest deep space reachable over nebula, within 250 tiles, never across the Dark Star's disc. It keeps one harbour per body of space at that distance. A spaceport with no deep space within 250 tiles has no harbour: it cannot trade or serve Destroyers, but it is still a paying stop on a trade network.

  • Freighters.

    • Odds. Every second each finished spaceport gets one chance per level to send a freighter. The odds rise after every miss (§5.2).
    • Where they go. A freighter sails to another player's spaceport that shares a body of space. You never trade with yourself, and never with a player either of you has stopped trading with. Destinations are weighted (§5.2): further than 300 tiles, among the nearest third, and friendly (ally or teammate) each add weight.
    • The voyage. It crawls overland at half speed to its harbour, crosses space at full speed, and crawls in at the far end.

  • Gold. On arrival both spaceports' owners receive the full payment, set by the tiles travelled (nebula and space alike):

    Tiles~100~200300~500~1,000
    Gold to each side5,20013,60052,50099,800125,000

    Short hauls pay very little: the payment rises steeply around 300 tiles, then grows by 50 a tile.

  • Raiders. A Destroyer can capture a freighter only in open deep space, never while it is on nebula or has touched shoreline space in the last 2 seconds. A captured freighter pays only its captor, at the captor's nearest spaceport. A freighter caught by the Hegemon's Destroyers is simply lost. A pirate corsair scuttles it.

  • Destroyers. You need a spaceport with a harbour on the body of space you click. A Destroyer launches from your harbour nearest the click and costs 250k → 500k → 750k → 1M (cap).

    • One per level: each level of a finished spaceport supports one Destroyer. With none to spare you cannot launch another. Losing a spaceport sinks nothing: the Destroyers you have stay, and you launch no more until you are back under your limit.
    • Repair: a Destroyer within 150 tiles of one of your harbours repairs 1 health a tick. Below 75% health it heads home.
    • Docking: a spaceport docks as many Destroyers as its level, sharing an extra 5 health a tick per level.

  • Level means more freighters, more berths, faster repair, and one more Destroyer.

  • Shared price count with Industrial Zones (§2.2).

3.4 Defense Post

Makes the ground around it expensive to take.

  • When an enemy conquers a tile within 30 tiles of your finished post, the attacker loses 5× the troops on that tile and the attack spends 3× its effort there, so the front moves about a third as fast.
  • On or off. A tile is covered or it is not: several posts do not stack, and posts cannot be upgraded.
  • Your posts defend your nebula only. An ally's post does not cover your tiles, and a post does nothing for unclaimed nebula.
  • The post works against the Hegemon's strikes too.
  • Fragile. A post is destroyed the moment its own tile falls; nobody captures it. A post upgraded with a Shield Array is not fragile (§3.6).
  • A post does not shoot.

3.5 Stargate

Fires troops or warheads at any tile in range.

  • Range: 180 tiles at level 1, +75 per level (255, 330, 405 …). No level cap. Level buys range only; the reload and the charge never change.
  • One charge, then a 5-second reload. A troop shot and a warhead launch spend the same charge.
  • Troop shots.
    • Size and target. The shot takes your attack ratio's share of your troops at launch, and may target any tile that is not yours, neutral nebula included.
    • Landing. It flies an arc and lands like a fleet: it takes the tile and fights outward, and the nebula is kept. If the tile became yours or a friend's in flight, the troops simply join.
    • Recall. A shot in flight can be recalled, troops home whole.
    • The Dark Star. A shot may land on the Dark Star, under the single-invader rule. It is refused at launch while another challenger holds the slot, and it turns back if the slot is taken in flight.
    • Nothing stops a shot. No defence intercepts it, and Shield Arrays do not keep it out.
  • Warheads. A Gamma Ray Burst or an Antimatter Reaction (5M each) launches from the nearest ready Stargate whose range covers the target. With none, there is no launch. Every launch flags the gate for a punitive strike for 30 seconds (§3.7).
  • Price never climbs: 500k for a new gate, 500k per level.

3.6 Shield Array

Upgrades a Defense Post: keeps Gamma Ray Bursts off everything within 100 tiles.

  • Built on a post. A Shield Array goes only on one of your own finished Defense Posts, and turns it into a shielded post: its icon replaces the post's. It cannot stand on bare ground. While the array is going up the post keeps defending; when it stands, it is the post.
  • The post's defence stays: within 30 tiles, attackers lose 5× the troops and advance 3× slower (§3.4).
  • The shield. When another player's Gamma Ray Burst lands, the blast does nothing within 100 tiles of the shielded post (a Gamma Ray Burst's own radius): no nebula lost, no troops killed, no building destroyed. The ground outside is cleared as usual, and a burst aimed at the post itself does nothing at all. The Hegemon's bursts are kept out like anyone's; your own are not.
  • What gets through. An Antimatter Reaction: aimed at the post, it erases the post's ground and the shield with it. Troops too: the Hegemon's strikes and every Stargate shot land inside. And the Hegemon's tithes.
  • Captured intact. Unlike a plain post, a shielded post taken by conquest is captured, shield and defence, and protects its new owner.
  • Construction. Nothing is shielded while the array is under construction.
  • Price. 2M flat on top of the post, one level, no upgrades. It counts as a post for the post's climbing price.

3.7 The cost of firing: punitive strikes

A warhead launch flags its Stargate for 30 seconds. In that window the Hegemon may answer:

  • The levy. Every living challenger, the launcher included, pays 25% of the troops they have at home, whichever warhead was fired.
  • The strike. The levy lands on the gate as a strike that captures it and razes everything within 34 tiles.
  • The pop. When the strike pops, the gate and every building it captured are destroyed.

An AI Hegemon punishes every flagged launch except from very small players (under 5% of the nebula).

3.8 Armies

Not a building, but every one is supported by an Industrial Zone.

A defensive unit that patrols your nebula and thins out attacks on you.

  • Supported by Industrial Zones: each level of a finished zone supports one Army. With none to spare you cannot deploy another. Losing a zone disbands nothing; you deploy no more until you are back under your limit.
  • Cost: 250k → 500k → 750k → 1M (cap), counted separately from Destroyers.
  • Deploying: click your own nebula. The Army sets out from your nearest finished Industrial Zone, walks there through your territory and patrols within 40 tiles of the spot, resting between moves.
  • Moving: click an Army to select it (Shift-drag to select several, Destroyers included), then click your own nebula to send it there. Armies never leave your territory and never cross deep space.
  • Firing: when the front of an attack on you comes within 40 tiles, an Army fires a shot over the nebula, at most once every 10 seconds. If the attack is still going when the shot lands, 10% of its troops die. Several Armies each fire their own shots. Every land attack counts: players, Lords, Barons, fleets and Stargate shots once they have landed, and the Hegemon's strikes.
  • Only defence: Armies never attack and never help an attack of yours.
  • Losing one: an Army has no health. It is lost when the tile under it is taken (nobody captures it) or a warhead hits it.
  • Newsfeed: every hit is reported to both sides: you see how many attacking troops your Army's shot killed, the attacker how many they lost.
  • Look and hotkey: on the map a pixel star in your colours; in the build bar three rank chevrons, hotkey 0.

4. Income compared

Working numbers at the default gold rate:

  • Base income: every challenger earns 6,000 gold a second (60,000 a minute); Barons half that. The Hegemon earns no base income.
  • The shadow tax: the Hegemon receives 3% of all gold every challenger gains, on top. Nothing is taken from the challengers.
  • One delivery on your own network with three of your own Population Centers on the route pays 30,000. The same route through a neighbour's three centers pays 75,000 to you and 75,000 to them; through an ally's, 105,000 each.
  • One freighter on a 300-tile route pays 52,500 to each side.

5. Odds in detail

5.1 Supply deliveries

Each finished Industrial Zone, at most once a second, rolls once per level with a chance of 1 / rate:

  • rate = (zones you own + 10) × 15 ÷ crowding.
  • Crowding depends on how many supply deliveries are under way in the whole galaxy. It favours the first deliveries (up to 1.5×), then damps the odds once the galaxy is busy, down to a quarter at most.

In an empty galaxy:

  • One level-1 zone sends about one delivery every 11 s.
  • Five level-1 zones send about one every 15 s each, one every 3 s in total.

5.2 Freighters

Every second each finished Spaceport rolls once per level with a chance of 1 / rate:

  • rate = 100 ÷ (misses since its last freighter + 1) ÷ crowding.
  • Crowding depends on how many freighters are at sea in the whole galaxy. It favours the first few hundred (up to 1.45×), then damps the odds once space is busy.
  • Destination weighting. Every eligible spaceport is entered once per level of the sending port. It is entered as many times again if it is at least 300 tiles away and among the nearest third, and as many again if it is at least 300 tiles away and belongs to an ally or teammate. One entry is drawn at random.

6. How Lords build

Lords run through these checks in order:

  1. Under land attack (not on Easy): they send their Armies to the front of the largest attack and deploy a new one there if a zone supports it.
  2. Under heavy land attack (incoming at least 35% of their troops): Defense Posts near the front, and nothing else. Easy Lords never build them; Medium ones half the time, one post; Hard and Impossible ones several.
  3. On a crowded map, first building: a Spaceport, or an Industrial Zone without a coast.
  4. Ready for the throne: they upgrade the Stargate that falls least short of the moat (up to level 5), or build one towards the Dark Star.
  5. By ratio to their Population Centers:
    • Spaceports 0.75 per center.
    • Industrial Zones 0.75 per center, or about 0.25 per center with a coast.
    • Stargates: the first at 0.4 per center, then 0.1–0.25 per center by difficulty, at most three.
  6. Otherwise a Population Center.

Other habits:

  • Upgrades. Past one building per 1,500 tiles they upgrade rather than build.
  • Saving up. They save towards one warhead (5M), and every building they own makes the next one look dearer to them.
  • Destroyers. They build Destroyers occasionally once they have a spaceport, within the same one-per-level limit.
  • One Shield Array. Once a Lord owns at least one of every other building (Population Center, Industrial Zone, Spaceport, Defense Post and Stargate), it shields one of its Defense Posts: the one whose shield would cover the most of its buildings. Only one.
  • Antimatter Reactions they keep for shields: a Lord fires one at a shielded post that shelters something it wants to hit, before any Gamma Ray Burst, and never at bare ground.
