The round

How a round of Under a Dark Star starts and ends - the opening launch, the timer, the killing blow, elimination and succession.

On this page
  1. Two ways to end
  2. Who sits on the throne
  3. The opening
  4. Elimination
  5. Why the rounds repeat

Two ways to end

A round ends in exactly two ways:

  • The Hegemon is eliminated. The Hegemon is out when it owns no tiles. The challenger whose attack takes its last tile wins the round and is the Hegemon of the next one.
  • The timer runs out (20 minutes by default). If the Hegemon still holds any tile, the Hegemon wins and keeps the throne.

There is no victory by owning a share of the map. However large your empire, the round runs through the Dark Star.

Who sits on the throne

The winner of a round is the Hegemon of the next. If no human holds the throne (the first round, or the previous winner has left), an AI Hegemon plays it. A solo game works the same way, unless you choose to play the Hegemon yourself. For how public lobbies handle the seat, see Thrones and lobbies.

The opening

At the start of a round every challenger is launched from the Dark Star to its chosen spawn. Nothing moves, no orders can be given and the clock holds until the last one has landed. Spawn protection starts at the landing.

Elimination

A challenger who loses all territory is out of the round. They may spectate, leave, or, where piracy is on, embrace piracy and sail on as a corsair with a chance to come back (see Piracy).

Only a challenger can eliminate a challenger. The Hegemon never can: its strikes become ruinously expensive against small players and stop altogether at a floor (see The Hegemon).

Why the rounds repeat

The Dark Star is a supermassive black hole at the centre of the galaxy, and whoever commands it commands the flow of time. When its enemies grow too strong, the Hegemon winds time back and resets them to weakness: that is every new round. Challengers have one round to break the throne before it happens again.
