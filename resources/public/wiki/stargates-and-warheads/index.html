Stargates, warheads and shields

Stargates fire troops and warheads in Under a Dark Star: the Gamma Ray Burst, the Antimatter Reaction, Shield Arrays and punitive strikes.

On this page
  1. Stargates
  2. Warheads
  3. Shield Array

Stargates

A Stargate fires your troops at any tile in range that is not yours.

  • Price. 500k gold, new or per upgrade. The price never climbs and there is no level cap.
  • Range. 180 tiles at level 1, +75 per level. Levels buy range only.
  • Reload. One charge, then a 5-second reload. A troop shot and a warhead launch spend the same charge.
  • The shot takes your attack ratio's share of your troops. It flies an arc and lands like a fleet: it takes the tile and fights outward, and the nebula is kept. If the tile became yours or a friend's in flight, the troops simply join.
  • Nothing stops a shot. No defence intercepts it, and Shield Arrays do not keep it out. A shot in flight can be recalled, troops home whole.
  • The Dark Star. A shot may land on the Dark Star, under the single-invader rule (see Overthrowing the Hegemon). Gates are the late-game road to the throne: they skip the moat.

A gate is the way past a front you cannot break, onto a coast your fleets cannot reach, and onto the Dark Star itself.

Warheads

WarheadPriceRadiusWhat it does
Gamma Ray Burst5M100 tilesClears the nebula, kills troops and buildings; shields stop it
Antimatter Reaction5M30 tilesErases the nebula into deep space; shields stop it
  • Launch. A warhead launches from the nearest ready Stargate whose range covers the target, and spends that gate's charge. With none, there is no launch. A warhead reaches only as far as a gate does, so range upgrades buy reach for warheads too.
  • The blast destroys territory, buildings, ships and fleets, the launcher's own included. Every building in the blast is destroyed outright, all levels.
  • A Gamma Ray Burst leaves the ground behind as neutral, irradiated nebula. An Antimatter Reaction leaves deep space: the ground is gone for good, and fleets can cross where it was. [[#shield-array|Shield Arrays]] keep both out.
  • The Dark Star is immune: warheads do nothing to its tiles.
  • The Hegemon fires warheads from the centre of the Dark Star, with no gate and no reload.
  • Warheads cannot be recalled once launched.

The price of firing: punitive strikes

Every launch flags its gate for 30 seconds. In that window the Hegemon may answer with a punitive strike: every living challenger, the launcher included, is levied 25% of the troops they have at home, whichever warhead was fired, and the levy lands on the gate as a strike that captures it and razes everything within 34 tiles. When it pops, the gate and every building it captured are destroyed. See The Hegemon.

An AI Hegemon punishes every launch except from very small players.

Shield Array

Built on a Defense Post, it keeps every other player's warheads off everything within 100 tiles.

  • On a post only. A Shield Array is built on one of your own Defense Posts and turns it into a shielded post. It cannot stand on bare ground. The post keeps its defence (attackers lose 5× the troops within 30 tiles); while the array goes up, the post defends as before.
  • Price. 2M gold on top of the post, one level, no upgrades. 10 seconds to build, and it shields nothing until it is finished. It still counts as a post for the post's climbing price.
  • What it stops. When another player's warhead lands, Gamma Ray Burst or Antimatter Reaction, the blast does nothing inside the shield: no nebula lost or erased, no troops killed, no building destroyed. The ground outside is hit as usual, and a warhead aimed at the post itself does nothing at all. The Hegemon's warheads are kept out like anyone's; your own are not.
  • What gets through. Troops: the Hegemon's strikes and every Stargate shot land inside, and conquest takes the post. And the Hegemon's tithes.
  • Captured intact. A plain Defense Post is destroyed when its tile is taken; a shielded post is captured, shield and defence, and protects its new owner.
