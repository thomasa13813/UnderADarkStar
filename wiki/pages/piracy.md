Elimination does not have to be the end of your round. Piracy is on in every public throne round;
a private lobby or a solo game may switch it off.

## Embracing piracy

- A challenger who has been eliminated may **embrace piracy** from the death screen, as often as
  they fall.
- The Hegemon, Lords and Barons never turn pirate.
- A pirate commands one ship, **the corsair**, and nothing else: no territory, no building, no
  alliances.
- Click deep space outside the Dark Star and its moat to launch the corsair, {{pirateWait}}
  seconds after your elimination.
- At sea the corsair flies no name and no colours: everyone sees only a pirate ship.

## Raiding

- Click deep space to send the corsair there on patrol; click nebula to send it ashore.
- On patrol it hunts **freighters**. Each one it catches is scuttled, the pirate pockets
  {{raidGold}} gold, and {{raidTroops}} troops go into the **hold**, which carries at most
  {{holdCap}}.
- It never fires on fleets but trades shots with Destroyers in range.
- It heals slowly anywhere in deep space and never docks.

## Beaching: the way back

Beaching the corsair claims {{claimTiles}} tiles around the landing for free (buildings
excluded) and sends the hold ashore as an attack on the coast's owner. **The pirate is a
challenger again**, in their own name and colours, with no spawn protection.

You cannot land on the Dark Star, on a player still under spawn protection, or on a teammate's
coast, and landings stop in the round's last {{landingClose}} seconds.

## Sinking

- A sunk corsair loses its whole hold; the raid gold stays with the player.
- The next corsair launches after a wait that doubles with every sinking, from {{pirateWait}}
  seconds up to {{pirateWaitCap}}.

## Winning as a pirate

A pirate at sea cannot win the round. A returned challenger can, like anyone. A pirate who leaves
the game has the corsair scuttled, and a returned challenger who falls again may embrace piracy
again.
